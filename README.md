## Project summary


Our project seeks to solve the problem of reducing the cost and expanding the range that accurate counts of whale populations can be taken. Current methods are costly or require counters to be within visual range of the whales they are observing which implies that they can only perform a count while near the shore or if the counter is riding a vessel on the ocean or a plane low enough to spot the whales as they count them. Instead, our system will use the sound emitted by whales such as their songs and calls to triangulate a direction from the microphones to the whale and analyze the recorded audio to estimate the distance to the whale or whales. By the end of this semester, we will have a prototype that using a deep learning model can determine the likelihood that a given audio recording is or is not a whale with a confidence of at least 70%, as well as to measure the distance and direction to the whale that produced the audio that was assessed to be a whale call/song.
